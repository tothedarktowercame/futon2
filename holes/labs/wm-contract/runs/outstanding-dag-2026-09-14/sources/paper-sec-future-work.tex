\section{Future Work} \label{sec:future-work}

%% Having gotten to know the system in the previous sections, the reader can now be formally introduced: borrow the title for the system from Deleuze and Guattari's account of forms of exteriority to the State apparatus \cite{deleuze1986nomadology}, and refer to the system as the \emph{War Machine}.  However, this name should not be taken to endorse militarization or violence.  ``Those who love peace must learn to organize as effectively as those who love war'' (Martin Luther King, Jr).

%% Inpsired by Deleuze and Guattari, the idea here is that agentic coding work should acquire lines of flight out of private model narration and platform enclosure into shared, inspectable resources: traces, commits, tests, folds, pattern catalogues, substrate records, and eventually governed public contributions.  The gates and typed records in this paper are therefore not only instruments of capture; read constructively, they are the apparatus that lets exploratory work become durable enough to join a commons.

A direct line of future work is to move from internal stack improvement to governed public contribution.
%% %The War Machine is a research prototype coupled with a design method, not a polished product for installation by a software team.
Its transferable principles are to keep decisions inspectable, score actions by named terms, distinguish recommendation from execution, require external evidence, and record honest gaps rather than hiding them.  The unimplemented \textsc{M-demonstration-foundry} mission reframes a daily scan not as ``find bugs and send pull requests,'' but as a commons-governed search for composable have--want arrows: places where our own needs, capabilities, or dependency paths meet a public repository's open issue, latent need, or already-existing solution. This matters because the same active-inference loop that selects internal work could select external work badly if it treats maintainer attention as a free resource. The foundry therefore makes ``no PR spam'' a rule-in-use: candidates must have endpoint evidence, bounded proof-of-work, tests, operator consent, and outcome logging before any outward act. In the best case, as happened in an interactive agential coding session that surfaced an emacs-websocket fix,\footnote{\url{https://github.com/ahyatt/emacs-websocket/pull/83}} the work is perceived by everyone as of mutual benefit: we need the repair, others need it too, and the contribution is valuable without pretending that agentic code repair alone is the result.

A second line of future work is a partial transplant of the catalogue into the \emph{harness} of a different agent family.  The FuLab runners \cite{corneli2026patternsnewgenerationai} asked language-model agents to enact the active-inference loop in-band --- maintain beliefs, weigh expected free energy, and narrate their own updates --- and in practice the formalism fought the agents rather than making them more effective.  The architecture described here places every load-bearing quantity \emph{around} the model instead: belief and observation are typed records, scoring is decomposed into named terms, and evidence consists of substrate writes the model cannot fabricate, and cannot work around.  The Zai harness instantiates this inversion at its memory seam with an immutable, bitemporally-indexed store: a deliberate memory is a typed R2 observation with provenance, not a rewrite of conversation history.  But queryability is not recall.  The live pilot wrote memories successfully while leaving them unattached to the pattern index, so a later agent would have to guess the right free-text query.  The AIF repair is to make recall part of R6 candidate construction: task context proposes a bounded set of patterns, and each pattern carries attached memories as inspectable warrants and counterexamples.  Selection records which memory ids were used; the independently witnessed outcome of acting on them updates belief and precision (R3/R7) and alone supplies R16 grounding.  Crucially, R6 must also admit ``none of the above'': a reason-bearing policy-hole plus the unmatched memories enters a mint lane, which proposes a new catalogue-shaped candidate at a broad, explicitly unearned prior.  This proposal is model expansion, not Bayesian Model Reduction; BMR can later merge or prune the expanded structure but cannot invent the new state.  A minted pattern earns standing only through R16-witnessed trials, never through semantic nearness, eloquence, or memory count.  Append-only provenance supports R9 by preventing the agent's assertion or later citation from certifying itself.  At R15's slower timescale, a Librarian revises memory--pattern attachments, reviews minted candidates, and submits accumulated structure to R17 reduction; similarity generates hypotheses, while model evidence and witnessed use dispose of them.  Thus ``what was recorded,'' ``what was retrieved,'' ``what was proposed,'' and ``what worked'' remain four separate, auditable claims.  The harness does not yet score this retrieval policy by expected free energy; its implemented scope is the typed write/read substrate and lexical addressability over tens of thousands of live evidence records. This suggests the catalogue's broader role as a checklist for making \emph{any} agent harness answerable.

The transplant also includes two proof-of-concept pieces beyond the memory seam.\footnote{The working record --- handoff packets, adjudication ledger, the labelled sample, and the measurement scripts --- lives in \srcref{futon2/holes/M-zaif-harness.md} and \srcref{futon2/holes/labs/M-zaif-harness/}.}  First, \emph{answerable delegation}: a driving agent ran a series of checked handoffs against a Zai worker, each adjudicated externally --- by re-running the work and spot re-fetching from the store, never by accepting the worker's report (R9 applied to agent-to-agent work).  Verdicts were recorded as typed evidence events, so the harness accumulates a claimed-versus-verified stream of exactly the shape the selection-gain controller (R14) folds over.  An audit of this paper could locate only four handoff packets and no six-row adjudication ledger, so the counts earlier reported here --- six handoffs, two of them failing and remediated on retry --- are withdrawn pending a primary verdict stream.  The mechanism is evidenced; its tally is not.  Second, \emph{measurement}: a probe asked whether the operator-interest signal that the stopping rule of \xprereg{} routes to is recoverable from a lexical correction detector over operator turns.  Two agents labelled a 120-item sample blind, disagreements plus a calibration slice went to the operator as a gold pass, and the answer is quantified: corrections are frequent (roughly a quarter of operator turns carry one) but the lexicon recovers only about a fifth of them at $0.42$ precision --- although when it does fire correctly, its routing between plan-, communication-, and world-directed corrections is essentially always right.  The design therefore requires correction and approval events to be \emph{declared} by the operator with an inline mark at the moment of writing (precision $1$ by construction), treats the lexicon as a reminder rather than an instrument, and reserves a detector trained over accumulating marks as the recall path.  \xzaif{} tabulates the transplant as built.

A fourth line sits one level up from any single proposer: laptop-based learning loops form a live genre, and the stack's record contains a worked analysis of the obvious lineage (\srcref{futon2/docs/futonzero-alphazero.md}) --- the ``board'' is the stack's structure, ``moves'' are patterns, a ``policy'' is a cascade, and the reward is a certified unit of earned capability --- together with the caveats that keep the analogy from flattering us.  What defines AlphaZero is not prior-plus-search-plus-value (that is classical game-tree search with a learned evaluation) but the \emph{closed self-play loop} with an incorruptible terminal reward, and by that bar the system here is a single-agent MDP closer to AlphaTensor. One candidate endogenous engine --- argument across competing possible-world buildouts --- was tested and \emph{falsified} against the realised policy-value floor: the dialectic did not beat the single best buildout. The implemented loop instead uses adjudicated fold outcomes to retrain the proposal reward, providing the search-result-trains-the-prior arrow (R2).  AlphaZero bought its loop with a datacenter --- a perfect simulator and millions of unambiguous terminal rewards; the laptop version substitutes patterns as good-enough invariants (evaluation without playout) and externally adjudicated fold outcomes (reward without a game), and the price of the substitution is that outcomes arrive in dozens per day rather than millions per hour.  Every empirical lesson in this paper's live sections is downstream of that price: label rate dominates proposal rate, small folds beat mission-scale ones, failed folds must be first-class records, and reward mis-specification must be caught by standing probes rather than statistical power.  The open question is not whether such a loop closes --- it demonstrably does --- but whether its curriculum can be made dense enough, at laptop scale, for the closed loop to outrun the hand-tuned baseline.  The capability-zones pattern of \xcatalog{} is the staged answer to \emph{where that curriculum should come from}: the partition of the embedded landscape gives ``what to learn next'' a measured ground (zone load, zone traffic, posterior variance) rather than a hand-ranked list, and the months of repository history it proposes to describe in capability terms are precisely the outcome corpus the search-result-trains-the-prior arrow has been waiting for.  Whether zone-guided learning actually densifies the curriculum is then an empirical question the loop itself can answer --- the same honest bar the falsified possible-worlds engine was held to.  A practical corollary belongs on the future-work ledger explicitly: the capability model must stay \emph{cheap to keep current}.  The hours-long embedding that first described months of repository history in capability terms is a one-time backfill debt, and the intended steady state rides three inexpensive channels --- trickle-embedding of the day's new commits on the existing nightly re-embed cadence, live posterior updates from enacted attempts (Beta arithmetic, no embedding at all), and streaming seed-centroid drift folded into \emph{discrete, operator-re-accepted partition generations} rather than silent mutation under the running controller.  A witness-bearing alternative --- indexing the same corpus as term postings in the hypergraph store, so that every zone assignment carries inspectable lexical witnesses --- is the candidate complement where cosine margins are too thin to call.  On a shepherding timeline this is future work of the best kind: small enough that it may well be finished before the paper is.
