\section{Discussion: turning the paper into one of its own agents}\label{sec:discussion}

While this paper was being revised, we applied its catalogue to the paper
itself.  The exercise is reported here because what it cost us is more
informative than the fact that we ran it.

\paragraph{The form.}  The manuscript was zipped into a claim graph in the
stack's AIF+ representation --- the same typed node/edge vocabulary used to
represent the research stack to itself --- with each claim carrying an external
referent: a source file, a commit, a cohort record, a citation.  A second,
mechanically generated ledger paired every file reference in the text with the
sentence making a claim about it, stamped with the referent's content hash.  The
graph was then vetted by three reviewing agents under the catalogue's own
discipline:

\begin{itemize}\tightlist
\item \emph{Author is not reviewer} (R9).  The author of the prose and of the
  claim graph was disqualified from issuing verdicts on either.  The reviewers
  were from a different model family, as the system's own author--reviewer
  separation requires.
\item \emph{Verdicts come from the referent, not the manuscript} (R12).  A claim
  checked by finding it restated elsewhere in the paper is Layer~1 --- internal
  consistency --- and was barred in advance from being reported as clearance.
\item \emph{Refute, do not confirm.}  Reviewers were instructed to default to
  \emph{unsupported} under uncertainty, on the grounds that a reviewer who finds
  support for everything has reported nothing.
\item \emph{Refutations become obligations, not edits} (the stop-line rule).  A
  refuted claim may not be resolved by softening the sentence until it is
  defensible; it enters a repair queue that the next revision must clear first.
\item \emph{Independent extraction.}  One reviewer was required to derive its own
  list of the papers' load-bearing claims \emph{before} being shown the author's,
  so that claims the author had not noticed could surface.  Reviewers can only
  refute what is listed; that asymmetry is the author's, not theirs.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What transferred, and what did not.}  This is the part that bears on
whether the catalogue generalises, and the answer is only half flattering.  Six
entries transferred to a non-code artifact without modification: typed
observation records (R2) became the referent ledger; no-self-certification (R9)
became the author's disqualification; two-layer calibration (R12) became the ban
on manuscript-internal verdicts; grounded actuation (R16) became the requirement
that a verdict touch the referent; interoceptive tripwires (R20) became content-hash
drift detection over the paper's own representation; and the stop-line rule
became the handling of refutations.

The scoring machinery did not transfer at all.  Nothing computed an expected
free energy over which claim to check next; the claims were enumerated and split
by topic.  R5, R6, R13, and R14 --- the canonical inference core --- played no
part.  So the \emph{apparatus} half of the catalogue carried over to a new kind
of artifact and the \emph{active-inference} half did not, which separates the
entries that are portable design moves from those that are descriptions of one
implementation, and does so by evidence rather than by our own maturity
labelling.

Asked why, we found a better answer than our own inattention: the target
representation has no slot for a policy.  It carries claims, conflicts,
preferences, a spine, and drift detection, and it ends each document with a
hand-authored \emph{next move} whose reasoning is nowhere recorded.  Across
fifty-two such files there is no scoring step of any kind.  An
active-inference-shaped audit could not have used the inference half of the
catalogue, because there was nothing there to put it in.  We have since
specified the missing layer and prototyped it over this paper's own repair
queue: declared preferences, a risk leg denominated in them, and an
outcome-uncertainty leg kept carefully unnamed as anything more.

The prototype disagrees with us.  Ranking the eight outstanding repairs, it
moved seven of them, and the disagreement is systematic rather than noisy: we
had put the cheap certain corrections first, and it puts them last, on the
grounds that their outcome is already known and doing them teaches nothing.  It
promotes instead the one piece of work whose result nobody can predict --- an
entry-by-entry census of which catalogue items have actually earned promotion
--- because the answer could change what this paper is entitled to call itself.
That is the exploration axis a severity sort cannot express, and it is the
argument our own capability-zones pattern makes about a codebase, arriving back
at us about a manuscript.

We report the prototype as a proposal and not a result.  Its inputs are
hand-set by the same author whose ranking it overturned, so what improved is
that the judgement is declared and split into two axes, not that it became
measured; and its ordering is a prediction that we are specifically not entitled
to confirm by re-reading it.

\paragraph{What mechanisation saw that reading could not.}  Every one of the
paper's file references resolves; there is no rot.  But the exercise could not
at first build a working drift detector, because the notation the paper used for
a file reference was also used for pattern identifiers, for named code
quantities, and for one path template containing a placeholder.  A checker built
on that notation would have reported eleven false failures and been correctly
ignored within a week.  The defect is invisible to a proofreader and was
invisible to us across several revisions; it became obvious the moment something
had to \emph{consume} the references rather than read past them.  We take the
general point to be that mechanising a claim changes what is knowable about it,
and that the change is not only in rigour but in what is \emph{visible}.

The repair was to split one macro into three --- a checkable file referent, a
pattern identifier, a named quantity --- which render identically and differ
only in what a machine may assume about them.  Sixty-eight referents now carry
content hashes, and a detector reports which of them have changed since the
manuscript last described them, naming the sections that must be re-checked.
That closes a loop the audit had just demonstrated was open: of the nineteen
adverse measurement verdicts, eight were pure staleness, prose that had been
true when written and had quietly stopped being so.  It also has a limit worth
stating in the same breath, because it is the same limit the whole exercise
keeps running into: a detector reports only that a referent \emph{changed}.  It
cannot report that a claim was wrong from the beginning.  The mis-cited test
file above sat unchanged throughout --- stable, and stably the wrong file.

\paragraph{What the independent extraction found.}  The reviewer tasked with
deriving its own spine produced a statement of the papers' central tension that
we prefer to our own.  Where we had named two conflicts --- that the work can
claim a running system but not a beneficial one, and that by the paper's own
promotion criterion most of the catalogue is still \emph{candidate} --- the
reviewer identified these as one boundary policed in two places: \emph{working}
must not become \emph{beneficial}, and \emph{implemented design move} must not
become \emph{promoted pattern}.  If either slips, the central claims become
self-certifying in precisely the way R9 prohibits.  It further identified six
claims doing load-bearing work in the argument without being stated outright ---
among them that independent evidence is not automatically independent
\emph{value} evidence, since a record can be external to the model that predicted
it while still measuring a proxy chosen by the system's authors --- and it
recommended that the thesis be narrowed accordingly.  We have adopted that
reading, and its reformulation of the thesis verbatim: what is demonstrated is
that catalogue entries, \emph{individually labelled candidate or promoted}, can
serve as inspectable control schemas in an AIF-backed harness --- operational
closure and auditability, not comparative benefit or generality.  The same
reviewer found four of our support edges to be rhetorical rather than load-bearing,
including the one carrying this paper's own title: since embeddings are by our own
account inessential to the live loop, their proposed future roles at the learning
frontier cannot support a thesis about the loop as it currently runs.  That
obligation is open rather than discharged.

\paragraph{The defect that reading could not have found.}  The single most
useful result was a mis-citation.  This paper's R-numbers are its own; they do
not align with the R-numbers of the implementation's separate AIF-completeness
contract, where R9 denotes named validation properties and R6 denotes softmax
action selection.  The footnote supporting \emph{No Self-Certification} --- the
pattern this paper describes as the one it would keep if it could keep only one
--- cited the implementation's \srcref{futon2/test/futon2/aif/r9_named_validation_test.clj} on the
strength of the matching numeral.  That file tests belief-variance shrinkage,
free-energy decrease, scoring stress, and abstention: the other scheme's R9.  It
contains no test of the mechanism our R9 actually specifies, which lives in a
different repository entirely.

The citation survived several revisions because it is perfectly plausible to a
reader: the pattern is R9, the file is named for R9.  Only something that had to
\emph{resolve} the reference, rather than read past it, could notice.  We regard
this as the exercise's clearest single justification, and as a caution that
generalises past this paper: two numbering schemes over the same subject matter,
maintained by the same people, will silently cross-contaminate citations, and
prose review will not catch it.

Before the verdicts arrived we recorded two expected refutations: the title
claim that embeddings are fundamental \emph{twice}, and the retention of a
learned proposer that had failed its preregistered comparison.  Both were
refuted, by two reviewers working independently on different lenses.  Of the
eight mechanism claims submitted, two were confirmed, five were judged
overstated, and one was refuted outright; the reviewer executed fifty-four tests
and regenerated the empirical ledger in the course of deciding.

The confirmations are worth stating because they were not foregone.  The claim
that the formalism lives outside the model was upheld against the actual prompt
construction: the belief map, the observation model, the ranked field and the
score decomposition are demonstrably absent from what the coding agent is sent.
The claim that the canonical scoring core is persisted separately from the
engineering controller was upheld against both the calculator and the trace
compactor.  Those are the two claims the architecture most depends on, and they
held.

What we did not anticipate is where the real damage was, and the pattern in it
is instructive.  The claim that an act's witness feeds the next belief --- the
conjunct that makes the loop a loop --- is not implemented: the actuator
snapshots, dispatches, logs and returns without ever calling the belief update,
and the path that does close that circuit runs through a human reading a morning
report.  The claim that the empirical section is generated from typed records
was refuted: the figure and ledger are, the surrounding prose is hand-maintained,
and two attempts were never deposited at all, so the census has holes where the
paper claimed completeness.  And the system was described throughout in a
present tense it had not earned --- see above.

The measurement review, arriving last, was the most punishing and the most
mechanical.  Working artifact-first --- treating agreement between two parts of
the manuscript as no evidence whatsoever --- it returned fifty-one verdicts, of
which nineteen were not confirmations.  The load-bearing experimental scores
mostly reproduced: the preregistered metrics, the simulation figures, and the
correction-probe numbers all recomputed from their generators.  What did not
survive were the counts around them.  Three were hard factual errors, two of
those in sentences the present authors had written during this revision,
including a wrong elapsed time in a \emph{section heading} and the wrong name
for the reviewing agent in the specimen run.  Seven further counts turned out to
be untraceable to any primary record --- not unchecked, but unsupported, and now
withdrawn from the paper rather than restated more carefully.  Eight were merely
stale, the empirical prose having drifted from the figure it stands beside while
the figure itself regenerated exactly.

One detail of that staleness is worth reporting because it cuts against us in a
useful direction: the outdated sentence was \emph{more pessimistic} than the
truth.  It said the last four attempts had all failed; the current ledger shows
one failure and three grounded successes.  We note this not to claim credit for
an accident but because a drift that had run the other way would have been the
more suspicious finding, and a reader has no way to tell which kind they are
looking at unless the direction is stated.

We draw a conclusion from that distribution which is uncomfortable and which we
think generalises.  We told the reviewers what we suspected, and they confirmed
what we suspected; that part of the exercise returned our own priors with a
citation attached.  Everything that changed the paper came from the parts we did
not point at.  Commissioning an adversary is not the same as being subjected to
one, and the difference showed up precisely at the boundary of our own
attention.

\emph{Disposition of the two self-implicating residuals (2026-08-31).}  The R9 close-step residual is now discharged by an independent closure-history audit that opened every cited artefact, found two false closures, and requires an artefact-changing commit and hunk for every closure.  The R16 residual is corrected rather than automated: a reviewer finding is an observation, while changing the obligation ledger is an adjudication.  The deliberate boundary is reviewer emission, accountable human consumption and disposition, then independent verification.  Automatically copying a reviewer label into the ledger would erase the accountable reader; this audit therefore does not claim an automatic belief update.

\emph{Yardstick disposition (2026-08-31).}  The score remains one pass, two partial and nine fail.  It is not raised by successful use of the audit, by War Machine declarations the audit does not call, or by surrounding registries and checks.  Moving the nine failures would require the audit itself to gain belief distributions, predictive updating, a forward model, softmax with abstention, adaptive reviewer precision, a complete executable property suite, unattended recurrence, budget-conserving reviewer coordination and a slower hyperparameter loop.  We withdraw the obligation to build that second controller here: the score remains an external diagnostic and the paper continues to refuse an active-inference label for the audit apparatus.

Read as a whole the ledger says something the narrative above softens: the
audit implemented the apparatus half of the catalogue with real but named
residuals, and implemented none of the inference half.  Its coverage was
therefore a matter of authorial choice throughout --- which is precisely the
property the missing half exists to remove.

\paragraph{Measured against a yardstick we did not write.}  There is an obvious
objection to the table above: it scores the audit against this paper's own
catalogue, which is the same circularity the audit exists to break.  The stack
happens to contain the right instrument for that.  Predating this work sits a
generic \emph{AIF completeness contract} --- twelve numbered requirements,
derived by diffing an implementation against the canonical literature rather
than against anything of ours, and written for a different project entirely.  It
exists for exactly our situation: its opening observation is that the
framework's vocabulary is ``evocative enough that it can be borrowed by code
which implements only a fraction of the apparatus,'' and each requirement is
stated so that it can be checked from the code rather than from the prose around
it.  Nine of the twelve are declared necessary for an honest active-inference
claim.

Scored against that contract, the audit apparatus satisfies \textbf{one} of the
twelve.  Its observation schema is genuine --- a fixed, named, normalised record
shape, the same at every row.  Two more are partial: the scoring prototype has
two terms, though only one of them is derived from anything, and the audit
leaves a reconstructible trail without that trail being a per-tick trace.
Everything else is absent.  There is no belief state carrying uncertainty rather
than a point label; no prediction, error, or update; no forward model, so the
scoring is exactly the heuristic the contract warns collapses out of its
absence; no temperature and no abstain; no adaptive precision, so the reviewer
that declared its own findings internal-consistency-only was weighted the same
as the two that executed code; no named validation properties of its own; no
scheduled operation; no coordination between three reviewers who duplicated each
other on the title claim; no second loop over its own preference weights.

We think that number is the single most useful output of the exercise.  It says
that what we built to audit this paper is an argumentation apparatus with typed
records and an honest attack discipline --- and that calling it active inference
would be borrowing the vocabulary for a fraction of the apparatus, which is the
practice the contract was written to stop.  The catalogue in this paper is a set
of design patterns; the contract is a set of requirements; and one of the
audit's incidental findings was that those two senses of ``R'' have been
colliding in our citations for months.

\paragraph{A second implementation, and one date that matters.}  The
representation used for the audit is not an apparatus built for it.  It is a
composition of two things that share an acronym --- the Argument Interchange
Format and the Active Inference Framework --- developed in this stack for an
unrelated purpose: representing the research programme to itself as an argument,
so that claims, conflicts and preferences could be inspected as data.  Its
design intent is an \emph{arena}, in which an argument competes against
counter-arguments; and it was built when this catalogue had far fewer entries
than it now has, which is why the active-inference half of the composition is
the half that never got built out.

We therefore looked at whether it independently instantiates any of the patterns
here, and one result is worth reporting because of its date.  That system carries
a drift detector which describes itself, in its own header comment, as an
\emph{interoception readout}: it compares each representation against the source
it was derived from and reports what has gone stale, so the apparatus notices its
own decay rather than having it discovered later by archaeology.  It was written
on 2026-05-03.  The pattern in this catalogue that describes that move ---
interoceptive tripwires, the machinery watching its own machinery --- was first
written down on 2026-07-16, ten weeks afterwards.  The instantiation precedes the
articulation, so it cannot have been built to fit the pattern, and the pattern
was not written to describe it.

That is the shape of evidence the promotion criterion actually asks for, and it
is worth being precise about how much of it we have.  It is one pattern, not the
catalogue; a second implementation, not a second author; a different language,
substrate and purpose, which is the part that gives it force.  It does not
promote anything on its own.  What it does is make the census we cannot yet
supply look tractable rather than hypothetical --- and, in the other direction,
that formalism already carries a per-claim evidence tally intended for exactly
this weighting, which is the mechanism this paper lacks and has been describing
as future work.

\paragraph{What this does not establish.}  The exercise is one application, in
one stack, by the same authors, using reviewing agents that we configured and
prompted --- including prompting them toward the two claims we already
suspected, which lowers the independence of those particular verdicts even as it
raises their yield.  It is a second \emph{domain}, not a second \emph{party}.  It
therefore does not discharge the candidate-versus-pattern conflict above: the
promotion criterion this paper endorses asks for repeated witnessed enactment
and review, and self-application supplies one further enactment by the same
hand.  What it does supply is a worked demonstration that the catalogue is
applicable to something other than the system it was derived from, an
identification of which half of it travelled, and a defect list we did not have
before.
