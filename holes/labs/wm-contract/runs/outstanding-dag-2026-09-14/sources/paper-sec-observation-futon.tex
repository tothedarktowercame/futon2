\xobservationheading\label{sec:observation}

This investigation began while revisiting the PLoP paper. We had described
an active-inference system, but close examination showed that we had not
established that the implementation was the system the account implied.
The difficulty was not only whether its parts worked. It was whether the
claimed operations occurred, whether their outputs reached the stated consumers,
and whether the resulting evidence warranted the larger claims. The deep dives
reported here turned that discrepancy into an explicit programme of work.

A working model needs two kinds of test: whether it still describes the world,
and whether acting on it achieves its purpose. The distinction is visible in a
war-room plotting table. Counters show a situation; their age tells the reader
how much confidence to place in it. A replay tests a proposed response. Neither
an up-to-date picture nor a successful rehearsal establishes that the response
has worked outside the room.

The War Machine is an active-inference harness around coding agents. Its
founding pattern, \texttt{WR-0}, asks for an open model, an available exit and a
verdict the apparatus cannot issue to itself. Auditing the implementation made
that last requirement concrete: recording a selection, producing an artifact
and verifying the intended outcome are separate achievements.

Our PLoP paper prompted this audit. Re-examining its claims revealed broken
handoffs and gaps between the architecture and recorded execution. This paper
reports the investigation and its consequences. It does not claim that the
repair programme has already produced a validated whole system. The issue
board records the current obligations; the following sections explain why they
matter and what evidence could discharge them.
