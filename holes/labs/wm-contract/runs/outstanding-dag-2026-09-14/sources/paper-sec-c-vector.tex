\section{Preferences over problems: making $C$ compute}\label{sec:c-vector}

\subsection{Outcome preferences}\label{sec:c-intro}

Here $C$ denotes a declared distribution over outcomes. It is distinct from
epistemic value, which concerns what the model may learn, and from the policy
prior $E$. The risk term compares a predicted distribution with $C$:
$\mathrm{risk}(\pi)=D_{\mathrm{KL}}[Q(o\mid\pi)\|C]$.
The comparison requires a common outcome domain; compatible numerical shapes
are insufficient.

The project's tetrahedral outcome model distinguishes nouns, verbs,
organisation and evidence. Its signature excludes preferences over evidence
content: it requires $v\neq\mathsf{evidence}$. Wanting a communicable witness
to exist is a different requirement from wanting that witness to report a
particular answer. That distinction must survive any richer outcome encoding.

Preferences may be indexed by horizon step, $C_\tau$. This permits preferences
about intermediate operation as well as terminal outcomes. It does not prove
that the implementation represents both. Consultation during scoring and
revision by an operator ruling are also separate operations: an extractor may
propose a revision with provenance, but cannot adopt it.

\subsection{What the channel model demonstrates}\label{sec:c-simple}

The recorded implementation uses thirteen per-channel healthy ranges, including
\texttt{sorry-count-norm} and \texttt{mission-health}. A demonstration dated
2026-09-08, with predicted variance $\sigma^2=0.01$, compares a stressed state
($0.85$, $0.2$) with a repaired state ($0.25$, $0.6$). Their two channel risks
are respectively $5.502+3.395$ and $0.194+0.455$ nats: a difference of $8.25$
nats favouring repair.

This establishes discrimination on those channel readings. It does not identify
which problem was solved or its value to the operator. Two problems with the
same readings receive the same channel risk. Preferences over problem identity,
solvability or benefit therefore require additional modelling and evidence.

\subsection{The disposition bridge and its unresolved domain}\label{sec:c-bridge}

A proposed bridge uses a conditional disposition model $P(d\mid o)$:
\[
Q(d\mid\pi)=\textstyle\sum_o P(d\mid o)Q(o\mid\pi),\qquad
\mathrm{disposition\mbox{-}risk}(\pi)=D_{\mathrm{KL}}[Q(d\mid\pi)\|C].
\]
This construction requires the predictor and kernel to mean the same thing by
$o$. It also retains the risk theorem's support conditions; an unobserved kind
cannot acquire invented mass just to make an evaluation finite.

The fit reported on 2026-09-08 contains one observed trajectory shape and one
outcome kind: three closed attempts, all \texttt{grounded-change}. Against the
ruled seed mass $\tfrac12$ for that kind, the calculation yields
$\ln 2=0.6931$ nats. It is constant across policies on that record and supplies
no selection discrimination.

A source check on 2026-09-09 found a separate problem. The fitted kernel reads
checkpoint trajectories; its cohort ledger contains no channel-valued
observation vector (\texttt{futon2/checks/disposition\_kernel.clj:20--27}).
The scorer supplies a channel-valued predicted mean
(\texttt{futon2/src/futon2/aif/efe.clj:701--703}). The constant fit masks this
mismatch. A justified relation between those observation domains is required
before the composition can support the intended claim. Merely substituting one
carrier for the other would change the question, not repair it.

\subsection{A proposed problem-level preference}\label{sec:c-problems}

Problem-indexed outcomes would let the operator distinguish which problems
should be solved, alongside how attempts end and how the process runs.
The declared axes include solvability, monetary benefit and societal benefit.
An evidence extractor could propose masses along those axes, citing its basis
in rulings, missions and recorded statements. A ruling would still be required
to adopt the proposal.

This is a design direction. It needs justified observations, support handling
and consumers before it can change a live selection. The existing channel
demonstration and constant disposition fit do not establish those obligations.
