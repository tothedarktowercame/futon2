## 1. IDENTIFY

### Motivation

The stack used to have something like a capability map in the old
`futon3/holes/features/*.devmap` files. It was low fidelity, per-repo,
and partly gestural, but it answered a real operator question:

> What exists already, what is half-built, and what is still only
> an aspiration?

That question has not gone away. The stack has since grown several
stronger but partial surfaces:

| Surface | What it sees well | What it does not unify |
|---|---|---|
| **Devmaps** | repo-local aspiration and prototype shape | cross-stack status and closure evidence |
| **Live invariants** | what the stack actually checks now | not the frontier, not the backlog |
| **Live violations** | what is currently failing | not the whole capability shape |
| **Candidate invariant queue** | promotion pressure | not what is already operational |
| **Sorry semilattice** | holes, closure pressure, aspiration debt | not a direct "what exists already" readout |
| **Mission geometry** | what work clusters with what | not capability status as such |
| **War Machine** | strategic visibility across the fleet | not a detailed capability substrate |
| **VSATARCS** | narrative explanation | not a queryable capability map |

Each of these is a projection over one developing map of stack
capabilities. None is yet the map itself.

### The actual sorry

> The stack has multiple good projections of capability, but no
> single printout that says, with provenance, "here is what exists
> already" and "here is what is still coming out of the bath."
>
> The operator can inspect live invariants, candidate invariants,
> mission topology, devmap aspirations, sorry pressure, and War
> Machine signals separately. The missing thing is a reconciled
> capability semilattice or DAG that pulls them together without
> flattening their differences.

This is not a complaint about any one surface. It is an integration
gap between surfaces that are already doing real work.

### Theoretical anchoring

- **Self-representing stack:** the stack should not only have useful
  surfaces; it should be able to say what those surfaces amount to
  together.
- **Structural law / invariant inventory:** the invariant surfaces are
  already the clearest place where the stack distinguishes what is live,
  what is violated, and what is still candidate.
- **Semilattice over tree:** capabilities in the stack are multiply
  supported and multiply pressured. One source can gesture at several
  capabilities; one capability can appear across several surfaces.
- **Promotion loop:** cleanup evidence -> candidate invariant ->
  operational family is already the closest thing the stack has to a
  capability-printer.
- **Stereolithographic framing:** the stack is accretive. "Exists
  already" and "coming out of the bath" are temporal/maturity claims,
  not just category labels.

### What "stack stereolithography" means here

The stack is developmental, not contemporaneous. Capabilities accrete.
Some are live and biting on the path now. Some are half-emerged and
visible through active work, partial wiring, or repeated strategic
pressure. Some are only declared in devmaps, candidate queues, mission
docs, or sorry registries.

M-stack-stereolithography names the job of producing a capability map
that can be read at those three states without pretending they are the
same thing:

- **exists already**: live, evidence-backed, operational enough to be
  honestly claimed
- **emerging**: partially wired, recently active, or strategically
  present but not yet fully operational
- **declared**: named by devmap, sorry, candidate invariant, or mission
  intention, but not yet live

The map should be semilattice-friendly: a capability may be supported by
multiple source surfaces, and one source surface may gesture at many
capabilities. The mission is not to force a tree where the data is not a
tree.

### The key claim

The devmaps, invariant surfaces, War Machine, sorry semilattice, and
mission geometry are not competing ontologies. They are different
readings of one developing capability landscape.

This mission exists to make that sentence operational.

### MAP stance: invariant-first

MAP for this mission should center on the invariant surfaces.

Why:

- the invariant split already gives the clearest state distinction in
  the stack: live / violated / candidate
- the invariant surfaces are the strongest existing attempt at a
  capability printer rather than just a dashboard or narrative
- the invariants are exactly where the stack most needs upgrading if the
  stereolithographic map is going to become functional rather than
  merely descriptive

So v1 should treat invariants as the backbone of the capability map:

- **live invariant families** are the clearest `:exists-already` rows
- **candidate invariants** are the clearest `:declared` rows
- **live violations**, mission work, devmap pressure, and sorry pressure
  explain where a capability is still being upgraded, repaired, or
  promoted into reality

The other surfaces still matter, but in MAP they should first be read as
evidence around the invariant backbone rather than as equal starting
ontologies.

### Scope in

- Define a minimal **capability node** shape that can be derived from
  existing artefacts without inventing unsupported structure
- Define a minimal **status vocabulary** for capabilities:
  `:exists-already`, `:emerging`, `:declared`
- Use the invariant surfaces as the first v1 backbone for that status
  split, then attach devmap / mission / sorry / War Machine signals
- Define provenance rules from current surfaces:
  - devmap prototypes and summaries
  - live invariant families
  - live invariant violations
  - candidate invariant queue
  - sorry entities / `:closes` relations
  - mission inventory and active-fronts / temporal signal
  - War Machine strategic signals
- Generate a first **printout** of the capability landscape as either:
  - a DAG-style report, or
  - a semilattice printout with typed edges and status bands
- Ensure the printout answers two operator questions directly:
  - "what exists already?"
  - "what is still coming out of the bath?"
- Preserve distinctions between source surfaces rather than hiding them:
  a capability row should say *why* it is marked live/emerging/declared

### Scope out

- Replacing the War Machine, stack geometry, or the invariant browser
- Building a new UI before the capability printout exists
- 3D or animated rendering in v1
- Creating a new speculative ontology with no evidence-bearing source
- Claiming a capability is live unless a current surface actually supports it
- Flattening live invariants, active violations, and candidate invariants
  into one undifferentiated bucket

### Proposed output shape

The first deliverable should be a generated printout, not a polished
viewer. The printout can later feed WebArxana, VSATARCS, or the War
Machine, but v1 should stand on its own.

Minimum row shape:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `capability-id` | stable name for the capability |
| `label` | human-readable name |
| `status` | `:exists-already` / `:emerging` / `:declared` |
| `evidence` | which source surfaces justify that status |
| `depends-on` | upstream capabilities or substrate |
| `gestured-by` | devmap / invariant / sorry / mission / war-machine loci |
| `notes` | concise rationale, especially when status is contested |

Minimum edge types:

- `:depends-on`
- `:realized-by`
- `:pressured-by`
- `:gestured-by`

If the data resists a strict DAG, the semilattice reading wins. The
printout may still render a DAG projection for the subset that is acyclic.

### Relationship to existing missions

| Mission | Relationship |
|---|---|
| `M-stack-geometry` | sibling and predecessor; gives topology, temporal signal, and the three-surface frame |
| `M-war-machine` | sibling; consumes the stack state strategically but should not become the detailed capability map |
| `M-a-sorry-enterprise` | sibling; sorry pressure is one input to "coming out of the bath" |
| `M-stack-inhabitation` | sibling; inhabited vs uninhabited surfaces are capability-state evidence |
| `M-hypergraph-operator` | downstream analytic reader over the same substrate |

### Why this is a separate mission from M-stack-geometry

M-stack-geometry asks where work sits relative to other work.
M-stack-stereolithography asks what capabilities the stack already has,
which ones are emerging, and which ones are still only declared.

Those are adjacent but not identical questions:

- geometry is primarily about neighbourhood, clustering, crossings, and
  temporal activity
- stereolithography is primarily about capability-state across multiple
  existing projections

Geometry can supply one projection into the new map. It should not have to
own the whole reconciliation problem.

### Completion criteria

1. A generated capability printout exists and is checked into the repo or
   reproducibly generated from current sources of truth.
2. Every printed capability is backed by at least one cited source surface.
3. The printout distinguishes `:exists-already`, `:emerging`, and
   `:declared` without collapsing them.
4. At least three current projection families are reconciled into the same
   capability substrate:
   devmaps, invariants, and sorry/mission state are the minimum set.
   Invariants are the required backbone for v1, not an optional input.
5. At least one capability is shown with evidence on both sides of the
   boundary: "already exists" in one surface and "still pressured" in
   another, with the tension made explicit rather than resolved by hand.
6. The operator can answer, from one printout, both:
   "what exists already?" and "what is still coming out of the bath?"
7. The War Machine, WebArxana, and VSATARCS are either:
   - all given a complete rendering over the same capability substrate, or
   - one rendering is completed and the mission closes with an explicit
     argument for why that rendering is sufficient at this stage and what
     remains for a follow-on version.
8. No capability is claimed as real merely because it was named in a mission
   doc or devmap; live claims must remain evidence-backed.

### Exit criterion for IDENTIFY

A human has read this proposal and agrees that:

1. the gap is real,
2. it is not already solved by any single current surface,
3. a generated printout is the right first artefact, and
4. the status split `exists already / emerging / declared` is the right
   first discipline.
