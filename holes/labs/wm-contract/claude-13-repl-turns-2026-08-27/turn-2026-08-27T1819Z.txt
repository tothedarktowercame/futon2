**The WM's analogue of C1 is false by construction.** "Every WM tick is stored in the Evidence Landscape" can't hold, because ticks aren't written there — they go to an ignored directory. So the end-to-end criteria can currently be stated for the operator half of the loop and not the machine half, which is backwards for a paper whose subject is the machine.

Three of those four rows are **one repair**: durability, queryability and annotatability all follow from writing a tick as an evidence record with an id instead of appending to a day file. Not a new mechanism — `family-fired`, `mission-sync-snapshot`, `memory-pull-use` and `process-alert` already live in the store, so non-conversational events fit there fine.

**The fourth row is a different question and I'd keep it separate.** No tick since 21 July. Whether the loop ran without tracing or didn't run is *not* established, and the two have very different consequences for what the paper may claim. Worth finding out before repairing the writer — the repair is pointless if nothing calls it, and misleading if something does.

On your broader observation: the two halves of today explain each other. Operator turns are stored, queryable and identified, so a defect in my *reading* was recoverable — I could re-query, re-parse, and find all three extraction bugs. WM ticks have no id, no query surface and no schema stability, so the same class of error wouldn't be recoverable; there'd be nothing to re-query against. **An apparatus that cannot be re-measured cannot be corrected.** That seems a better argument for the repair than "the paper needs the numbers."
