`grim trigger` is the repeated-games name for exactly what `p1-action` does: cooperate until the counterpart defects once, then defect (here, abstain) forever, with no path back. One snatch and P1 is out for the rest of the game. It's a fine baseline, but it's hardcoded — which is why the collection can fire all it likes and nothing happens.

So let me build the alternative: a policy whose action is *derived* from the patterns that fire.Built it. `pi-patterns` sits alongside `pi-grim` in `checks/playout_snatch.clj` (`33209b9`), and its action is the THEN of the highest-precedence pattern that fires — so editing a pattern changes what P1 does.

Three things had to become data for that to work:

- **THEN clauses.** Play-grain entries now carry `:then` (situation → action) and `:precedence` for conflict resolution. Four entries deliberately have none: ask size, the talk channel and the free mark are real game features this model doesn't represent, so they stay advisory rather than getting an invented action.
- **Denouncing.** `revert-then-invert` records that G4's AutoJudge reverts *then* inverts, and that denouncing is **optional** — so it's an action, and `:repair-observed?` is set by that event. Previously it was set by `judge-available?`, i.e. a judge existing stood in for a repair having happened.
- **A score**, from the payoff rule the README already records: exchange of n nets +n, snatched −n, denunciation +3n.

```
scenario         grim   patterns   difference
g1  snatcher       -1         -5          -4
g1  sharer          5         15          10
g1  cautious        0          0           0
g4  snatcher       -1          3           4
g2  snatcher      -10        -10           0
g5  sharer          5         15          10
```

**G4 is what the collection was written for.** Grim takes one loss and leaves. The pattern policy denounces, gets the transfer inverted, re-enters on the observed repair, and finishes +3 against grim's −1. Two patterns produce all of it, and the hardcoded policy couldn't reach either.

**G1 against a snatcher is where the collection is wrong,** and this is the part I'd not have got by reading. The pattern policy loses 5 where grim loses 1, because after the first snatch nothing tells it to stop — `consult-the-remedy-before-exiting` requires a remedy to exist, G1 has none, so `exchange-when-both-sides-gain` keeps firing and P1 keeps feeding a player who takes. **Nothing in the collection covers exiting an arrangement that offers no recourse.** That's a seventh gap, and the first one found by playing rather than by reading — which is the argument for having a score at all.

G1/cautious ties at zero but the two policies differ: grim offers into a refusal five times, while `an-unmodelled-response-stops-the-line` fires in round 2 and the pattern policy stops. Refusal is free here, so the score can't see the difference — a limit of this payoff model, not of the pattern.

One correction to yesterday's numbers: **G1's four coverage gaps were an artefact.** I fixed two guards for echoing the action; the field itself — `:offer-made?`, meaning "P1 has already decided to offer" — was the problem. With the policy deciding first, patterns describing the situation couldn't fire in abstained rounds. No guard now mentions the round's action, and G1 goes to zero gaps: an exchange *was* available in those rounds, which is precisely why the policy needed a pattern telling it not to take one.
