**And your test forced a third polarity, which is the real gain.** The scenario note had accepting (operator doing it right) and refusing (recorded failure). The naive-fix test adds: **refusing — the plausible fix**.

So family 5's refusing fixture isn't "a mission outside the domain." It's **the whitelist with a fifth entry added** — a change that looks like a fix, passes any test that only asks *is this mission in the list?*, and must still be refused because the domain is enumerated rather than derived.

> A contract that refuses only broken inputs is a type-check. A contract that refuses the *tempting wrong answer* is a spec.

Each invariant now carries its own tempting wrong answer: I2's is *log it* (a log line isn't a value the fold can read); I3's is *report it more prominently* (emitting into the record is what already happens); I4's is *regenerate the artifact* (stale again in twelve weeks, and nothing says so); I5's is *make every proposer stamp its id* — which fails because **a proposer that emits nothing stamps nothing**.

Five lines, each naming a class rather than a case, each with a dated violation and a plausible fix behind it. That's a claim about what the machine won't do again — which is what Tier 0 was for.
