**"A semilattice rather than a tree" isn't a mathematical contrast.** A rooted tree *is* a meet semilattice — any two nodes have a greatest common ancestor. I had run together two different properties: *overlap* (some pattern stands on two authorities — the negation of the tree condition, which is what *A City Is Not a Tree* argues about) and *being a meet semilattice* (any two patterns have a greatest common authority). **On semilattice vs BV vs Markov: they're not alternatives, they type different objects.**

- The **authority structure** — what the acting patterns stand on — is a finite poset. Order theory.
- The **trajectory** — patterns co-firing within a round, rounds in sequence — is a BV term: `copar` within a round (they hold together, no order), `seq` between rounds, which is *non-commutative* and therefore states that this round preceded that one. The poset has no time in it.
- The **uncertainty** — which pattern acts given a distribution over dispositions — is a Markov kernel.

`DarkTower/BV.lean` already has the constructors. The theorem worth proving is that the G4 trajectory term is not congruent to a reordering of its rounds — exactly the claim `seq` exists to make, and exactly what a bag of patterns loses. Registered, not attempted; it needs a negative result about the congruence relation, which is a real proof rather than a `decide`.

**Your finding is in.** Holding the collection and the policy fixed, the counterpart's disposition selects which sub-structure of the collection a run inhabits — four distinct shapes across six situations. That's stronger than the score difference, because it says what part of the library a situation reaches rather than how well it did.
