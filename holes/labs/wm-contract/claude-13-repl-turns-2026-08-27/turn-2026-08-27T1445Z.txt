**So the main design consequence: the build must not depend on `@how`.** It takes typed edges from *any* source and is indifferent to which directive supplied them.

**Five steps, only two of them per-domain:**

1. **support** — a `core.logic` relation run forward against domain facts *(the facts are domain-specific; the relation shape isn't)*
2. **mass** — a Beta posterior per **edge**, `credit-for` generalised per-edge as `aif2/tension.clj` already did per-entry
3. **kernel** — `node → D(node)`; a cascade is Kleisli composition
4. **readouts** — `discriminates?` and `entropy`
5. **mirror** — every witness ships a case that must find **zero mass**

**And it belongs in R5 for a real reason, not by filing convenience.** R5's requirement is that the evaluate stage report what its criterion set does *not* cover. Step 4 makes that computable: a channel's entropy **is** the statement of how much the criterion fails to determine. R5 is where a spread becomes reportable rather than merely present.

**Four slices drafted for codex-22**, each with a numeric acceptance test:

- **S1 · edge-graph reader** — acceptance is it reports 60 / 38 / 1 over the library. The measured counts *are* the test.
- **S2 · kernel constructor** — acceptance is it reproduces the same four bindings the existing witness returns, Beta(1,1) on unattested edges, entropy `log 4` on a uniform four-point channel.
- **S3 · snatch adapter + mirror** — witness has positive entropy, **mirror has zero mass**.
- **S4 · mathematics adapter** — *held* for the apparatus repair, noted because it's the domain that will really exercise this.

S1 and S2 are independent and can go in parallel; S3 needs both.
