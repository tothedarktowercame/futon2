That's the Kangaroo reading exactly: an LRU evicts what hasn't been touched; a terminal-only criterion set evicts every action whose payoff isn't terminal.

**And `:declined-ground-unknown` gives the membership criterion.** It *is* still `:declined` — the decline is the critical point, the unknown ground is a refinement that changes no downstream action. So:

> An event type earns its own place iff its distinction leads somewhere different. Otherwise it's a refinement, and belongs as a **typed absence** on its parent.

Applied: `-on-merit` vs `-on-capacity` — **yes**, *"you cannot consult these buyers into buying"* versus *"gated on resources"*, different offers follow. `:silent` vs `:unknown` — **yes**, *we asked and got nothing* versus *no offer was ever made*. `:declined-ground-unknown` — **no**; it's `:declined` with the ground marked absent. So vocabulary A isn't too small, it's **mis-shaped**: a flat slot where a parent-plus-absence belongs, which is precisely what makes a decline uncountable *as* a decline.

One thing worth knowing about how we got here — `lights.bb` says it in its own comment: the `other` slot exists because finer classes *"would otherwise each demand a hue nobody can tell apart from its neighbour."* **The granularity of the event ontology was set by how many colours a reader can distinguish.** A defensible figure decision that quietly became the vocabulary.

So a bigger vocabulary needs three things rather than more entries: **structure** (B is a chain, A is a list — a chain states preconditions and can't be optimised terminally), **parent/refinement with refinements as typed absences**, and **the membership test**, so it grows by consequence rather than by noticing a case.
