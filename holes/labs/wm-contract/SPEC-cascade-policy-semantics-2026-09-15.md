## 2. Proposed finite generative signature and quantities

At one construction stage, for finite nonempty state S and observation O, control domain U, finite horizon T, and a nonempty duplicate-free finite family Π of admissible **(pattern cascade, precedence) pairs**, use the repaired existing `ProbabilityKernel`. Parameters are observation kernel A, initial distribution D, controlled transition kernel B, preferences Cτ, and positive habit weights E on Π. S and O may be product spaces. No outcome alphabet, tension, curvature, or empirical parameter is fixed here.

The central interpretation maps each admitted pair π to `Bπ : ProbabilityKernel S S`. The patterns that can fire are `firing(π) = precedence.filter (· ∈ nodes)`, in that order: precedence may be a wider, temperament-level order naming patterns outside this cascade, and those never fire here; nodes that precedence does not list (for example the authorities organise adds by up-closure) supply no control. At s, p*(s) is the first pattern in firing(π) whose guard holds, and Bπ uses B^p*(s); if none fires, Bπ is the identity at s, as A1/A4 specify. The policy is scoreable iff every pattern in firing(π) has an interpretation; otherwise it is a typed refusal, never a skipped or default pattern. Grounds: the Snatch exemplar (`F12SnatchExemplar.lean:33–88`) has precedence `[5,2,0,3,6,10,20]` naming 5, 3, 6 and 20 outside its nodes `{0,2,10} ∪ {7,11,18,22}`, and its acting patterns `[10,2,0]` lie in the selected nodes; rejecting precedences that mention non-nodes would reject that record. (Resolved by claude-20 on codex-26's 1b-i blocker.) Guard evaluation on hidden state is explicitly EXTRAPOLATION 3, not a claim of runtime correspondence. Candidate pairs are outputs of problem-conditioned progressive construction, not a fixed authored subset; see the Stage-0 contract below. For comparisons, T is a common declared prediction horizon for the candidate family, not each candidate’s pattern count. A pattern-count/application budget is separate from this comparison horizon; see the horizon-fairness ruling below. Nesting follows A5.

D is the initial-state prior; after observed data, prediction begins with declared current inferred beliefs q₀ (D only at initialization). One application precedes each scored observation: `qτ+1(s′|π) = Σs qτ(s|π) Bπ(s′|s)`, `Jτ(s,o|π) = qτ(s|π) A(o|s)`, `Qτ(o|π) = Σs Jτ(s,o|π)`, and τ=1,…,T. Thus the initial pre-control observation is not counted as a control consequence. Outcomes include target discharge; the concrete alphabet, distributions and inference algorithm remain parameters/separate work.

For each τ use **exactly** `PreferenceRiskSeparation.riskAdmissible Qτ Cτ` (`PreferenceRiskSeparation.lean:45–49`): positive predictive mass at zero preferred mass makes the finite score inadmissible; zero predictive mass contributes zero. Do not smooth away zeros. Proposed canonical quantity:

`Gτ(π) = KL(Qτ(·|π) || Cτ) + Σs qτ(s|π) H(A(·|s))`; `G(π) = Στ=1…T Gτ(π)`.

**Partial-cascade contract.** Let x be the problem and retained context, c_k the cascade at construction stage k, and Γ_k its current interpreted composition and grounded model inputs. Define a family `G_x(c_k; Γ_k)` using the same joint-derived EFE law above. Construction index k is NOT prediction time τ and is NOT T. A partially constructed cascade must be scoreable without requiring its eventual completed successor. An empty or incomplete construction is not rejected merely for incompleteness: its present effects, unfinished target and available interpretations must have declared prediction semantics. Missing essential model inputs remain typed missing inputs, never an invented zero, completed successor, default kernel or “infinite bad score.” The requirement is to supply those inputs for each partial state; a refusal is not successful fulfillment of that requirement.

When there are several proposals, choose only from strictly improving ones; how proposals are generated and ties between improving choices are resolved remains explicit. Stop extension when no available admitted proposal strictly improves, while recording the searched family and its coverage. This is a local stopping condition, not a proof of global optimality, nor an instruction to stop merely because the first proposal was worse. Stopping construction does not assert target success or authorize enactment/yield. Do not use a nonzero-temperature draw to accept a worse extension under an “iff decreases” claim. The existing common-T result is one mathematical comparison interface, not a decision that every construction step adds a scored observation. Blindly setting T=k adds nonnegative terms merely because construction grew and cannot express the desired improving-extension test in general. Conversely truncating predictions, padding with invented zero costs, or subtracting a length term is not justified. A stopping-time/episode formulation is an alternative to be argued with examples, not adopted here. ## 3. Acceptance theorem statements (no proofs in this packet)

1. **Well-formed predictions:** normalized nonnegative initial beliefs, Bπτ and A yield normalized nonnegative qτ, Jτ and Qτ at every τ≤T, with duplicate-free support and zero off-support mass preserved.
2. **Risk domain:** a positive Qτ mass at preferred zero contradicts the existing riskAdmissible predicate; zero-Q terms contribute zero. Under admission all terms in the displayed finite G are defined without substituted preferences.
3. **Derived decomposition:** under those domain conditions, for each admitted π,τ, `Gτ = Lτ − Iτ`, hence `G = Στ Lτ − Στ Iτ`. Derive from J and its marginals; do not take the equality or estimator agreement as a hypothesis.
4. **Information and score bounds:** Iτ≥0; with discrete finite A, ambiguity≥0 and KL≥0, hence Gτ≥0. These are categorical results, not differential-entropy claims.
5. **Policy prior:** compare only a common predeclared T/context/observation/preference schedule as required by §2; finite nonempty Π, E positive on Π and γ>0 imply a positive partition function and normalized prior for the displayed base, habit and precision forms (and the explicitly combined extension). Equal habit weights and strictly smaller G imply strictly larger prior mass. This does not assert posterior ordering when F differs.
6. **Composition control:** construct a finite model and two admissible cascades containing the same patterns whose agreed composition semantics yield different Qτ and different summed G. Neither a precedence-indicator score nor a pasted G value qualifies. This is a mathematical example, not live evidence. It must continue to separate under any subsequently adopted length-fair formulation; averaging independent per-pattern scores fails this requirement.
7. **No-interaction control:** construct a model under explicit independence/invariance conditions giving equal **per-step observable distributions and ambiguity contributions**, hence equal summed G, for the compared legal compositions. Commuting transitions alone imply neither this premise nor equal cumulative G; terminal equality alone is insufficient.
8. **Singleton:** a one-pattern, one-step interpretation yields its controlled one-step q₁/Q₁ and G₁, and the cascade sum equals that one-step score. Do not erase residual effects by claiming this for arbitrary T.

9. **Length fairness and aligned composition (new acceptance obligation; not yet proved):** reject comparisons of raw sums at differing T_pi. At common T, equal per-step Q and ambiguity imply equal G regardless of pattern counts. For a concrete composition of two well-specified patterns, prove from their kernels that each preserves Q_tau=C_tau on every reachable compared step, including after composition; the component and composite risk sums then all equal zero, so the composite is no worse than either component on risk grounds. To assert the analogous zero-G result, additionally derive zero expected ambiguity at each step. These are model properties to prove, not free “well-specified” Boolean flags or assumed score inequalities. Include a positive-epsilon/unequal-horizon negative control. No unconditional non-increase theorem for arbitrary composed kernels is claimed.

10. **Stalling and compounding controls (new, unproved):** at a common target/context/horizon, derive a non-firing board's failure outcome and compare it with successful progress. Separately construct two ill-specified patterns whose interaction worsens outcome risk relative to either alone, deriving the inequality from their kernels; include a cancellation/saturation counterexample to prevent an unjustified universal claim.
11. **Attestation twins (new, unproved):** resolve the concrete attestation inputs above; refuse missing/invalid records. Where evidence supports different kernels or uncertainty, derive their predicted joints and test a predeclared risk ordering in a concrete twin experiment. Include a metadata-only twin invariance control: unchanged model and preferences imply unchanged G. A strict ordering of metadata-only twins is blocked, not promised.

12. **Partial-state and progressive construction (Stage-0 acceptance, not yet proved):** evaluate initial, intermediate and complete constructions from the same EFE definition with their actual model inputs. Retain per-problem generated candidate families and exact parent/delta identities. Prove successful extension admission equivalent to legal scored ΔG<0; demonstrate decreasing, increasing and equal-score proposals, including interaction-dependent changes. A refused/uninterpreted partial state is a missing requirement, not a passing case. Demonstrate that construction-step count is not used as an independent cost. Record local-search coverage and distinguish stopping from success. These tests must use model-derived predictions, not a ΔG predicate supplied as a score assumption.
