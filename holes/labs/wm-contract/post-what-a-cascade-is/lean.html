In the moduleWhat it says
ReadingA list of fragments, each a list of citations of library patterns. It has no other field: there is nowhere to put a list of cascades.
UnitAn identity within its cascade, a library pattern with proof that it is in the library, and optionally where it came from in a reading (fragment and position). A unit cut from the pattern graph has no reading provenance.
CascadeA nonempty finite set of units, a set of precedes edges and a set of overlap pairs between its own units, and a rank for each unit that every precedes edge must strictly increase. The rank is what rules out cycles.
readingCascadesA function from a reading to its cascades, in two modes, both of which go into the policy set. Units are citations; every unit of one cited fragment precedes every unit of the next cited fragment. Alternatives picks one citation per fragment in every combination; overlap keeps all, with those in the same fragment overlapping. Proved about its results: every unit has reading provenance, and two units overlap exactly when they share a fragment.
GraphEdge.authoredDirectionDirection is a recorded field of a graph edge, or absent. It is never derived from names.
RetractionSpecThe contract the retraction computation must meet: every retraction contains every seed it was given; its labels are library patterns; each precedes edge follows a graph edge authored in exactly that direction; each overlap comes from a graph edge with no authored direction; no seeds gives no retractions; at most k are returned.
structuralIdentityWhat makes two cascades the same policy: their patterns and relations, ignoring provenance and rank. This matches the code, which compares mission, pattern sequence and edges.
PolicySet, constructThe constructor of a policy set is private. The only way to obtain one is construct reading graph params: the reading’s cascades in both modes, then the retractions of its usable seeds, keeping the first cascade of each structural identity.

Proved: every member is a valid cascade; members have pairwise distinct structural identities; every member contains a pattern the reading cites; an empty reading gives an empty policy set; the number of members is at most the number of alternatives cascades plus the number of overlap cascades plus k; with k = 0 the policy set is exactly the deduplicated reading cascades.

Controls that fail as they should: calling the policy-set constructor by name or with structure notation from outside the module; giving a reading a list of cascades; a cascade with a cycle, with a label outside the library, or with a unit overlapping itself; proving that a graph edge with no authored direction justifies an arrow.

One real policy set, checked. A script (futon2/scripts/wm_policy_set_to_lean.py) writes the M-distributed-proofreaders example from this page’s data into Lean: the eight fragments with their three citations, the eight graph edges its retractions use, and the three retractions as explicit five-unit cascades. Lean then establishes:

  • the reading yields one cascade in each mode, each of three units and two ordering edges, and they are one structure;
  • each retraction contains every cited pattern that has an edge in the graph, and every one of its overlaps comes from a graph edge with no authored direction;
  • the policy set has four members: the reading cascade and three distinct retractions, as the code reports.

Three planted errors make the build fail, as they should: deleting a citation from the data; turning a retraction’s overlap into an arrow while its graph edge has no authored direction; adding a unit whose pattern is outside the library. Two of the theorems (the two counts after duplicates are removed) are checked by compiled evaluation, native_decide, which rests on an additional soundness axiom of the Lean compiler; the rest use only the standard axioms. The exported data has no edge weights, so the generated graph gives every edge weight 1; no theorem here depends on weight.

What trying the real example found. Three defects in the definitions, none of which a fixture had shown: the first version let a reading carry its own list of cascades, which was the old free list one level down; the second could not express a retraction at all, because it required every unit to have a fragment; the third removed duplicates by comparing more than the code compares. And one fact about the code: its “reported” cascade counts are inflated by annotations (see below).

What the Lean does not say. The retraction computation itself is not defined; it enters as a named function under the contract above, sourced to pattern_retraction.clj, and the example checks three of its outputs against that contract, not the computation. Nothing requires a policy set to have more than one member; a run whose policy sets are degenerate is caught by the run-facts check and the alert, not by this module. Nothing says the fragments are the operator’s own words. And none of this is connected to selection: G, F and the choice among policies are specified elsewhere and still refer to the old definition.
