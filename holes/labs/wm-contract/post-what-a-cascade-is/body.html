Why this post exists

On 29 and 30 September the War Machine ran twenty clicks in which the set of policies it chose among had one member. The Lean model did not object, because its definition of the policy set, cascadePolicySet (menu : List …), takes the list as an argument that nothing has to produce. Every theorem about it holds for any list, including a list of one. Joe’s word for that argument was a backdoor.

Since then a replacement has been built in pieces: read the target’s text, form cascades from the reading and from the pattern graph, score them. Along the way a question came up about pooling cascades that share a first step. Joe’s answer was that the question is premature until we can see what these cascades are, what they do and what their specification is. This post is the attempt to show that, using the seven missions for which a reading exists.

Working definitions

These are the definitions the code implements today, in plain words. A Lean version, in which the policy set can only be obtained from the construction, is reported in the next section.

TermDefinitionThe countable thing
LibraryThe finite set of pattern ids in futon3/library.1,431 patterns.
TargetSomething the machine could work on: a mission, a ticket, a proposal.288 in the field today; 199 have a file to read.
Reading象’s analysis of a target’s HEAD section (or its opening, when there is no HEAD): the text split into fragments, each with a role and with the library patterns it cites and the ones it considered and rejected.7 targets have one. It is keyed to a digest of the exact text read, so an edit to that text makes it stale.
UnitOne citation: a pattern at a fragment. The same pattern cited in two fragments is two units.1 to 16 per reading cascade below.
CascadeA nonempty set of units, each labelled with a library pattern, with two kinds of relation between units: precedes (directed; must contain no cycle) and overlap (undirected; the two belong together and neither must come first). The units with nothing preceding them are the roots.{{NPOL}} across the seven targets.
Reading cascadeA cascade taken directly from a reading: units in fragment order, each preceding the next. Where a fragment cites several patterns, either one is picked per fragment (“alternatives”, giving several cascades) or all are kept with overlap between them (“overlap”).1 to 3 structurally distinct per target.
Pattern graphA graph over the library. Two patterns are joined when they were cited together, cited in successive turns, rejected beside each other, or when one pattern’s own text names the other in its “why” or “how”.{{PIN}}.
RetractionA small connected subgraph of the pattern graph that contains the reading’s patterns (the seeds), found as a cheapest tree joining them. It adds connector patterns the reading did not cite. The tool returns the k cheapest.k = 3 by default. A seed with no edges cannot be joined and is counted as a failure.
PolicyA structurally distinct cascade for a target. Two cascades that differ only in annotation are one policy.
Policy set of a targetThe output of a construction: the reading cascades together with the retractions seeded by the reading, deduplicated. Its inputs are the library, the reading, the pinned graph, k and the edge weights. There is no other way to get a policy into it, and a target without a current reading has an empty policy set and a counted failure, not a substitute.1 to 6 per target below.

What a cascade does

Selection scores each policy by imagining it carried out. The imagined execution goes in rounds. In each round, every unit whose predecessors are all done is attempted, and each attempt succeeds with that pattern’s success probability. So the arrangement decides what can happen when: a chain of three units needs at least three rounds, while three units with no ordering between them can all finish in the first. With success probability one half, three unordered units are all done after one round with probability 1/8; a chain of three, with probability 0. Codex-6 has proved these two figures in Lean for the three-unit case.

In the drawings below, each row is the earliest round in which a unit can be attempted, and roots are shaded green. Black arrows are precedes; blue dashed lines are overlap. On retractions each arrow is labelled with the kind of graph relation it came from.

Two numbers are then computed for each policy:

  • F, fit to the present. How well the reading supports the cascade’s patterns for this text: a pattern 象 cited for the text is given probability 0.9 of fitting, one it rejected 0.1, one never read against the text 0.5, and F is the average surprisal of those, plus a term for how many fragments the cascade covers. The three probabilities are stated preliminary values, not measurements. Connector patterns added by a retraction were never read against the text, which is why retractions have a higher (worse) F.
  • G, expected free energy. Risk plus ambiguity minus expected information gain, over the imagined rounds. Risk measures how far the imagined progress falls from what is preferred; the preference used at the moment is simply “more units done is better, and the last units done is better still”.

Lower is better for both. The selection law combines them with habit: probability proportional to exp(log E − F − γ·G).

A reading of something that is not a HEAD

Joe pointed this one out. The HEAD section of M-web-arxana-ui-improvements contains only the sentence “The mission’s live operator-shape, captured before IDENTIFY hardens it into a tractable gap statement.” That is the template’s definition of what a HEAD is; the mission has no HEAD. What the examples show that is wrong or unsettled

  1. Most retractions are unordered bags. The pattern graph is undirected except where a pattern’s own text names another. Until 30 September the scorer turned every graph edge into an arrow pointing from the alphabetically earlier pattern name to the later one; that is fixed (futon2 3a247262a): an edge with an authored direction keeps it and every other graph edge is an overlap. In the 16 retractions here, 22 of 98 edges have an authored direction. The consequence is visible in the drawings: most retractions now have no ordering at all, every unit is a root, and the imagined execution attempts all of them in the first round. G rewards that. A five-pattern bag scores 0.89 to 1.04 where the chain read from the same text scores 1.66 to 2.05. Whether five patterns can really be applied at once, and so whether this is what a cascade should be allowed to be, is not settled by anything in the model.
  2. “Cited in the next turn” is treated as having no direction. Many graph edges are of kind next-in-session: one pattern was cited in a turn and the other in the following turn. That relation has an order in the evidence, but the graph stores it as undirected, so it becomes an overlap.
  3. Wide cascades cannot be scored. The exact scorer enumerates every subset of the units attemptable in a round, so its cost doubles with each additional one: measured at 0.4 seconds for five, 1.6 for nine, 2.9 for ten. The three M-war-machine-aif-completion retractions allow 13 or more at once and exhausted memory. Since futon2 86822f367 an arrangement wider than nine is refused before any enumeration and counted as a failure; nothing approximate is substituted. So at present the machine cannot consider a wide cascade at all.
  4. Retractions of one seed set are near-copies. For M-self-documenting-stack the three retractions differ by one connector. Claude-17 measured that asking for 6 or 10 gives 6 or 10, all at nearly the same cost, with no sign of settling. So k decides how many near-copies a target gets, and k has no stated source.
  5. Most of these readings are not of a HEAD at all. When a mission file has no HEAD section, the request script reads whatever comes before the first section heading: usually a title, a status line and some framing, of unknown authorship. Of the six readings here, M-distributed-proofreaders and M-metric-harness are of a real HEAD; M-essays-diachronic-model, M-self-documenting-stack, M-war-machine-aif-completion and M-value-creation-loop are of the file’s opening. Of 199 mission targets with a file, 15 have a HEAD with content, and none of the 55 tickets does. Joe’s proposal, now being piloted, is to recover HEADs from his own recorded words in the Evidence Landscape: over the first 20 missions tried, 7 have his words commissioning the mission at or before its creation, 9 have his words about it from later, and 4 have none.
  6. A definition of a HEAD was read as a HEAD. See M-web-arxana-ui-improvements above. Nothing between the mission file and the policy set checks that the text read is the operator’s own statement. The Lean definition of a reading does not require it either.
  7. Every success probability is one half, because no pattern has recorded trials. Expected information gain is therefore the same (0.31) for every policy, and habit is neutral for every policy. Differences in G come from risk and ambiguity only.
  8. The preference is synthetic. “More units done is better” was written for this scorer. It is not the preference from Joe’s ruling of 22 September that the real decision scores with, nor the one derived from mission wants that the real decision records without scoring. Which of the three should score cascades is an open question for Joe.
  9. Coverage. Seven readings exist. The field has 288 targets; 192 missions with a file have no reading, and 89 targets (tickets, proposals) have no file the machine knows of.
  10. Not in the formal model yet. Until the Lean definitions above are complete and the real decision is changed to call the construction, the old free-list definition is still the one the running machine answers to.
