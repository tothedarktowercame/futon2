## 4. The three things that separate the arms

**(a) Arm (i) closes O1 and does not close O2 or O3.** Widening `Cascade`
(`Holes.lean:29-35`) with `selected` and `admittedBy` makes O1 writable of what
`organise` returns, and `armOneO1` proves it on the recorded 20 nodes — the
union is not the C59 fixture's `selected ∪ ∅ ∪ ∅`, since 9 of the 20 come from
`admittedBy`. But the widened carrier still has **one** edge field, and O2 asks
that an organised edge have *authored* reachability. There is no authored
relation in the carrier, and the only one in the vocabulary is
`Repository.standsOn` (`Holes.lean:123`). So `armOneO2` and `armOneO3` take the
repository as an argument: arm (i) does not stand alone, it needs arm (iii)'s
pairing for two of the three laws it was supposed to fix.

**(b) Arm (ii) is the only self-sufficient carrier, and it buys an O4 that
cannot fire.** `CascadeDiff` (`Holes.lean:846-858`) carries `authoredEdges` and
`organisedEdges` as separate fields, so O1–O3 need nothing else — they are
already proved of `wmZaifCascadeDiffFixture`. Two costs, both measured rather
than argued. First, the retyped signature needs a `Score` type parameter that
the s3e signature at `Holes.lean:861` does not have
(`armTwoOrganiseType`, `F12D1Arms.lean:119`). Second, the extra law the wider
carrier makes room for is O4, and on the only library-scale run recorded its
antecedent is false: `precedenceBefore = precedenceAfter` (`:123`),
`actingOrderBefore = actingOrderAfter` (`:129`) and `scoreBefore = scoreAfter`
(`:134`), because `construct_cascade.clj:402` (fields at `:420-421`) sets both
precedence vectors to `[]` for this run and carries no score. This is a fact
about the run, not about the shape of the statement — on the C59 fixture the
same pair does move (control in §5), and slice 3 (C541) established separately
that no recorded cascade has two rule-carrying members, so no library-scale O4
witness exists to swap in.

**(c) Arm (iii) proves O2 and O3 and cannot write O1 at all.** The pair
`Cascade × Repository` gives O2 and O3 directly, and both are proved
(`armThreeO2`, `armThreeO3`). O1 is a different matter: `Cascade`
(`Holes.lean:29-35`) has no `selected` and neither carrier has anything for
`admittedBy` — `Repository` is `patterns`, `standsOn`, `acyclic`
(`Holes.lean:121-124`). The one substitution the pair makes available is
`Repository.patterns` for `selected`, and it is **refuted on the recorded data**
rather than left as a doubt: `armThreeO1PatternsSubstitutionFails`
(`F12D1Arms.lean:180`) proves the cascade's 20 nodes are not the 27-vertex
closure, with vertex 26 the witness. So under arm (iii) O1's third origin has
nowhere to live, which is the same gap `:LA2` amended `CascadeDiff` to close
(`Holes.lean:833-845`).

**What no arm does.** None of the three states all four laws of one carrier
without either an extra argument (arm i, arm iii) or a type parameter the
interface does not declare together with an O4 that no recorded run can satisfy
(arm ii).

## 6. Two defects found in this slice's own work, and repaired

**The dispatched arm file understated what it proved.** Its report said all
three before/after pairs are equal on the zaif run, but only the precedence one
was in a theorem statement; the acting-order and score equalities were `have`s
inside a proof that never used them, so two thirds of the claim was not
machine-checked by anything a later reader could cite. Repaired in
`e77f73b679`: they are now `armTwoZaifActingOrderFlat` and
`armTwoZaifScoreFlat`.

**The checker recorded every declaration as present.** `(some? (str/includes?
…))` is true whatever the source says, because `(some? false)` is true. The
third plant is what caught it — a plant designed to test the arm file found the
defect in the instrument instead. Fixed in this commit, with the reason written
at the call site.
