# W2 — pushout sketch: what bigger problem do the solved problems solve?

Commissioned by Joe, 2026-09-06 (compressed): survey the solved problems
and "think about how they might push out... a co-limit to a bigger overall
problem... building up a grid until we say, this is the big problem we're
solving with the futon stack — or three or four or even seven independent
ones. The different futon repos are each meant to solve a problem, but what
happens when we put them together? Do they solve a bigger problem? Or is it
just something that keeps me busy?"

Inputs: W1 survey (+correction: 11 summaries), A1-A5 problem nodes, the
Box-12 process census, the 20-node WM problem map, C537.

## Four clusters, each a pushout along a stated common ground

A. RECORDS CARRY WARRANT (or teach nothing).
   Members: M-first-flights HEAD ("records carry their derivations, so
   error has structure to propagate into"); P4 completion-prose-without-
   execution-evidence; the PA11z trim finding (conclusions retained,
   warrant discarded); unqueryable-trails-block-recovery;
   undeclared-cascades-cannot-be-audited; S1 silent transport loss.
   Ground: an assertion severed from its derivation cannot be checked,
   learned from, or recovered.

B. ONE QUERYABLE SELF-ACCOUNT.
   Members: S2 four-vocabularies-one-world; P2 provenance-bearing
   projection; P1 self-observation-lags-actuation; S3 unused-
   infrastructure-yields-no-fitness-evidence. The paper's Box apparatus is
   this cluster solving itself.
   Ground: knowing what exists and its state, with provenance, at stack
   scale.

C. FEEDBACK REACHES EVERY PARTICIPANT.
   Members: Box-12 :operator-turn cells (3 of 7 stages are Joe);
   agent-discoveries-do-not-teach-humans; the trainers' equity family;
   S4 understanding-did-not-update-the-argument;
   peripheral-hops-reset-self-regulation.
   Ground: the loop's human/stakeholder edges are where learning silently
   dies.

D. ACCOUNTABLE NEXT-ACTION CHOICE, INCLUDING GOAL FORMATION.
   Members: the WM core (G over policies; C; find/organise), L1
   policy-conditioned-EIG, L2 grain compliance, R11 budget arbitration,
   effort-estimation, C537 pre-goal preference (C_ser).
   Ground: deciding what to do next, accountably, under uncertainty —
   with the goals themselves formable, not given.

## The apex candidate (pushout of pushouts)

Each cluster is a precondition of ONE loop: B is observation; A is the
likelihood structure that lets error propagate; C is enaction + preference
feedback across all participants; D is the selection core. Apex, stated
once:

  CAN A COMMUNITY OF HUMANS AND MACHINES DOING OPEN-ENDED WORK LEAVE AN
  ACCOUNT OF ITSELF STRUCTURED ENOUGH THAT THE WORK CAN STEER ITSELF --
  SO THAT ACTIVITY BECOMES INFERENCE RATHER THAN MOTION?

The keeps-me-busy worry has a sharp form here: busy-ness with unstructured
records, no self-account, and no participant feedback is action with no
error propagation. Evidence the apex is real rather than decorative:
independent projects (IRC transport, flight records, training equity, Lean
witnesses) fell into the SAME four holes without coordinating -- repeated
independent rediscovery is what a genuine common problem looks like.

## Caveats and residue

- The project-domain problems (equity, ai4ci, ukrns) join through C as
  INSTANCES: the grid explains why the stack helps them, not why they
  exist. If the apex is rejected they survive as independent problems --
  Joe's "three or four or even seven" fallback is clusters A-D plus the
  project domains.
- Entertainment is inside the model, not outside it: C537's pre-goal
  preference says dwelling on interesting questions is legitimate C mass.

## Mechanization path (offered, not dispatched)

Building the grid IS organise at problem grain: spans become authored
edges between problem nodes, cluster nodes and the apex become committed
nodes with `source-class: synthesis` (a new class, visible to the strict
gate), and the colimit is an organise run over the problems namespace --
F12's operation, O1-O4 laws applying unchanged. That would make "what
problem does the stack solve" a certifiable graph object rather than an
essay.
