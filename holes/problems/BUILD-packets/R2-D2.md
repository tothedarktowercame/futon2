## The trap this packet exists to avoid — read it as an acceptance clause
`Channel` must be read from the **source declaration** `observation-channels` (`observation.clj`, def at
:11). It must **not** be derived from the trace — not as a union of observed keys, not as the modal key
set, not "the keys of record 1".

**Why the obvious justification for that is wrong, and what the real risk is.** An earlier draft of this
packet said a corpus-derived `Channel` makes the contract "true by construction". That is false, and the
correction matters. Measured over this corpus, all three derivations give **identical** results:

    Channel := source-declared (14)  ->  fires on 2, passes 790
    Channel := trace-UNION     (14)  ->  fires on 2, passes 790
    Channel := trace-MODAL     (14)  ->  fires on 2, passes 790
    (union == modal == declared, on this corpus, today)

A union-derived checker still fires on the two short records. It gives **the right answer for the wrong
reason**, which is worse than a tautology because it looks like it works — and it stops working silently
the moment a channel is added or removed, which is exactly the schema drift this record exists to catch.

**The discriminating test, which your fixture must contain — a fixture CORPUS, not a fixture record.**
Point the checker at a fixture *directory* whose records **all** carry a fifteenth, undeclared key. Then:

    source-keyed Channel (14)   ->  fires on ALL of them
    union-derived Channel (15)  ->  passes ALL of them

I have run this: the two outcomes are `[5 fires, 0 passes]` and `[0 fires, 5 passes]` on a 5-record
fixture corpus. They are opposite, so the test discriminates in both directions. A single synthetic
record appended to the real corpus does **not** discriminate — a checker that derives `Channel` from a
fixed corpus path and takes the record as a separate input fires on it correctly while remaining
self-certifying on the real data. That is the natural shape for a bb script with a default corpus path,
so it is not an exotic evasion; the corpus is the thing that must vary.

**And make the claim auditable rather than inferred from behaviour: the EDN report must name the
provenance of the `Channel` it used — the file and line it was read from.**

## Reader — mandatory, and one warning
Loop `clojure.edn/read` over a `java.io.PushbackReader` to an EOF sentinel. **Never `edn/read-string`**,

## `likelihood` — what is being asked, and the way it could be a facade
`P-R2` `solved` (1) wants `likelihood : Channel → Option …`, `None` for the six, **as a type**. Today the
partition is a declared *set* (`channels-with-likelihood`, `belief.clj:925-926`) plus a comment —
R2-D1's phrasing, which I verified: `:n-a-by-design` appears **once in the whole source**, as a comment
at `belief.clj:925`, and never in `observation.clj`.

**The facade to avoid.** Writing `(if (contains? channels-with-likelihood c) (Some …) None)` re-encodes
the same set under a new name and is **total by construction** — it can never fail, and it would be a
type substitution in the other direction: a set copied across a file boundary and called a type.

**What makes it a genuine typing: totality checked in the direction that can fail.** Enumerate the six
`None` channels **explicitly** in your source — `:consulting-pct :depositing-signal :loop-health
:mathematics-pct :portfolio-pct :stack-pct` — and have the checker assert that this enumeration equals
`observation-channels ∖ channels-with-likelihood`. Then a channel added to `observation.clj` and to
neither list makes the assertion fail, which is the drift that will actually happen. Do **not** compute
the six from the difference and then compare the difference to itself.

**Do not invent likelihoods for the six** and do not reclassify any channel; if you think a channel is on
the wrong side, that is a finding for the owner.

## Out of scope — do not attempt
The **turn channel** (P-R2 `solved` (2)) is blocked on a design decision and R2-D1's refusal to choose a
normalisation stands and was correct. **Stated by kind, not by name: do not add any channel whose value
is derivable from turn presence or turn count alone, under any name.** The record's objection is
*presence, not content* — a property of the quantity, not of the key — so forbidding
`:operator-turn-count` by name would leave `:turn-signal`, `:acknowledged-ratio` and
`:operator-engagement` open, and each would recreate the same defect.
Related and worth knowing: `:acknowledged?` has exactly one producer in the corpus, a hard-coded `true`
at `src/futon2/aif/lane_futility.clj:333` for synthetic nags — my own earlier packet said "expected:
none" and R2-D1 correctly falsified that. Do not build on it.
